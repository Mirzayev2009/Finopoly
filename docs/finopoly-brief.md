Each team starts with $100,000 in capital and faces the real challenges of financial history. Our aim isn’t just to play a board game, but to show you how big world events affect markets, and how smart thinking can turn challenges into opportunities.
You’ll work in teams, moving around the board, making quick investment choices, and reacting to surprises. Before each round, a major historic financial event will be announced. You’ll have 25 minutes to research what happened and, most importantly, find out which stock sectors or investments soared, and which crashed.
At every event, focus your research on finding:
Which industries, companies, or types of assets performed BEST?
Which sectors, stocks, or investments lost the most value?
Which business and money strategies were smart decisions in that era?
What were the warning signs before the crash or boom?
How did government action (or inaction) change the market?
Your goal is to use those details to guide your team’s investment strategy, so every dollar counts and every choice brings you closer to winning. Remember: quick teamwork and a clear eye for opportunity make a great investor. Good luck and enjoy discovering how simple—and exciting—finance can be!"

Team Roles for Finopoly
1. The Market Analyst
Focus: Research and Information
Responsibilities:
During the research phase, lead the effort to find out which sectors, stocks, and assets performed best or worst during the assigned event.
Compile concise facts and figures about industry trends, company winners/losers, and relevant news.
Share findings with teammates, ensuring everyone understands the key points for strategy.
2. The Investment Strategist
Focus: Decision-Making and Planning
Responsibilities:
Listen to the analyst’s research and propose a clear investment plan for each turn.
Compare risk vs reward and suggest how much to invest in each opportunity.
Make final choices during the 1-minute quick decision rounds, ensuring the team’s money is used wisely.
3. The Risk Manager
Focus: Portfolio Tracking and Defense
Responsibilities:
Keep track of the team’s cash, investments, gains, and losses each round.
Monitor the portfolio to avoid overexposure to risk and warn teammates about risky moves.
React quickly to negative cards or events, seeking ways to minimize losses, access safety options, or lobby for team protections.

Example Workflow During Each Phase
Research: The Market Analyst summarizes findings for the team.
Strategy Discussion: The Investment Strategist weighs options and recommends moves.
Quick Actions: The Risk Manager ensures all investments comply with team goals, keeping balance and hedging when needed.

Event Briefings & Specific Research Questions
Event 1: The Great Depression (1929)
"For this round, you’ll research the Great Depression—the time in history when stock markets crashed and the world economy slowed down. What did businesses, banks, and regular people do to survive? Did some investments or companies do better than others? What strategies helped people recover? Your job is to get the big picture and think about how you’d make decisions if you were an investor at that time."
"Research these questions for your team strategy:
Which stock sectors and companies collapsed fastest after the crash? (e.g., banks, railroads, manufacturing)
Did any types of investments do better, like utilities or government bonds?
Who managed to protect their wealth—were there recession-proof stocks?
Were there businesses that survived or even grew while others shrank?
What would you avoid investing in at that time, and what would you buy if you had $100,000?"

Event 2: The 2008 US Financial Crisis
"Next up is the 2008 crash. Ask yourselves: What caused it? How did banks, companies, and everyday people respond? Which sectors collapsed, and which found ways to bounce back? What role did the government play in helping the economy recover? Try to spot patterns—safe investments, risky moves, and how smart investors survived tough times."
"Focus your research on:
Which stocks or sectors lost the most value (e.g., banks, real estate, mortgage lenders)?
Were there companies or sectors that went up, or bounced back quickly (e.g., technology, consumer essentials)?
How did government bailouts affect specific stocks and industries?
What was the impact on bonds, gold, or cash?
With $100,000, which assets proved to be safe and which were risky?"

Event 3: Trump vs. China Trade War
"Your team should explore:
"Now, imagine a global trade war. What are tariffs, and how do they affect international business? Which companies gained advantages, and which lost out? What happened to confidence in the markets? Your team should follow how businesses adjusted their strategies, searched for opportunities at home or abroad, and how real investors reacted to uncertainty."
Which companies or industries were hit hardest by tariffs and trade tensions? (e.g., manufacturing, agriculture, tech)
Did domestic-focused stocks outperform exports?
Which Chinese or US stocks went up or down during this period?
Were there sectors or countries that benefited or lost out from shifting global supply chains?
What investments became attractive as a result of government policies?"

Event 4: Asian Financial Crisis (1997)
Finally, the Asian Financial Crisis. Why did currency values suddenly plummet, and what happened to banks and companies? Which industries suffered, and which managed to recover quickly? How did investors and governments respond to sudden risk? Focus your research on quick changes, where money moves fast, and what steps helped stabilize markets.
"To prepare a winning strategy, research:
Which assets and stocks collapsed in value during the currency crisis? (e.g., Asian banks, real estate, export businesses)
Were there international investments or sectors that remained stable—or even grew?
How did changes in currency rates impact specific companies or types of stocks?
Which countries recovered fastest, and why?
If you had $100,000 to invest before or during this crisis, what decisions would you make to protect or grow your money?"

"Be specific—look for actual company names, stock sectors, and types of investments. The details you uncover will help guide every move your team makes in Finopoly!"
